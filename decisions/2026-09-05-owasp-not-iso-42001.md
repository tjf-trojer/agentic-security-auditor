# Decision: audit against OWASP ASI01-ASI10, not ISO/IEC 42001

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Status:** decided, implemented

## Context

An auditor for AI agents could plausibly enforce ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management
systems), the NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act, or the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic
Applications. All four are real, citable, and in current use.

## Decision

OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 (ASI01 to ASI10) as the sole spine.
The EU AI Act is a conditional second anchor. ISO/IEC 42001 is excluded entirely.

## Reasoning

**ISO 42001 is excluded for a licensing reason, not a substantive one, and the
reason is disqualifying.** ISO standards are copyrighted and sold. The text
cannot be redistributed, so it cannot go in `reference/`. An auditor that cites a
standard a reader cannot open is an auditor whose findings cannot be checked,
which is the failure this whole repository is arranged to avoid. The constraint
is worth stating plainly rather than working around: **if the standard cannot
ship, it cannot anchor.**

**NIST AI RMF is public domain and could ship, but it is a risk-management
framework rather than a checklist of failure modes.** Its functions (Govern, Map,
Measure, Manage) describe how an organisation should organise itself. They do not
give an auditor a set of things to look for in a system prompt and a tool list.
Auditing an artifact against it would produce findings about the organisation,
not about the artifact.

**OWASP fits the artifact.** Ten named failure modes, each with common examples,
attack scenarios and prescribed mitigations, written specifically about agents
that hold tools and take actions. Every category maps onto something visible in a
definition. It is also CC BY-SA 4.0, so it ships whole.

**The EU AI Act is kept, conditionally, because OWASP has a gap it cannot close.**
OWASP says a thing can fail. It does not say who is answerable when it does. For
the reader this auditor serves, the person signing off a deployment, that second
question is often the operative one. So the Act is available behind the scope
gate and dropped when it does not bind, which for most agent definitions it does
not.

## Consequences

- Anyone wanting ISO 42001 conformity gets nothing from this repository, and the
  README says so rather than implying coverage.
- If OWASP publishes a 2027 edition, `scripts/verify.py` will fail on drifted
  provisions rather than silently citing the old text. That is the intended
  behaviour: a human should decide what a new edition changes.
- The Act's presence must stay honestly conditional. Both real audits in
  `examples.md` conclude it does not bind. If a future example stretched Annex III
  to manufacture a legal hook, this decision would have been abandoned in
  practice while still standing in writing.
